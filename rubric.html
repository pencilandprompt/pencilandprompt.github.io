How to tell what’s wrong
with your colored pencil portrait

Eight checks. Each one is pass or fail — not a score out of ten. A 7/10 tells you nothing you can act on; “the away-side plane finally turns, but it went grey” tells you what to do tomorrow.

Every line below came out of a real critique of a real drawing. An AI has graded one hand-drawn portrait a week on this channel since May 2026, and the recurring findings were deduplicated into ten rules. This page is those rules turned into something you can hold your own drawing against. The failure lines are not hypothetical — they are mistakes made here, on paper, and logged.

The eight checks

1

Value

R1 · R3 · R10

Is the darkest shadow actually as dark as the photo — or darker?

Passes when — Shadow depth matches the reference's real contrast, and the lit planes reach a true peak highlight with a hard value jump — forehead apex, nose bridge, cheekbone.

Most common failure — The moment a shadow note is given, the shadows overshoot past it. Under soft frontal light this carves deep hollows the photograph never contained.

Recurring ×5 — the most persistent thread in the log.

2

Temperature

R2 · R10

Are the shadows warm, or have they gone grey?

Passes when — Shadow masses carry warmth glazed into them — olive, sienna, oxidised warmth. A shadow still receives bounced light.

Most common failure — Neutral grey or ashy darks. A dead-grey shadow reads as an absence of light rather than a turned form. Usual upstream cause: a dark cool-grey underpainting, which optically deadens warm skin to mud.

Validated ×3, reopened once.

3

Edge

R9 · R4

Is there one sharpest point, or is everything equally sharp?

Passes when — Sharpest at one focal accent (usually the eyes and lash line), softening across cheeks and jaw, edges lost at the hair, boundary and garment.

Most common failure — Uniform detail everywhere. It reads flat-focus and directs the eye nowhere. The discipline is actively de-emphasising the non-focal areas, not merely softening the background.

Recurring ×2, Validated ×1.

4

Structure

R4

Does the away side of the face turn as convincingly as the lit side?

Passes when — The receding plane stays a mid-value, warm plane carrying one crisp core-shadow edge where the cheekbone turns.

Most common failure — Either a soft dark blur that never turns — or, when overcorrected, a heavy cool mass stuck on the cheek.

Validated ×1.

5

Unification

R5

Is the skin one surface, or a patchwork?

Passes when — Each skin colour built in thin, even passes across the whole zone before any local deepening.

Most common failure — Building area-by-area, then trying to rescue it with one closing glaze. Unification is a method, not a final step.

Closing — open five weeks, essentially resolved.

6

Calibration

R1

Did you fix the note, or overshoot it?

Passes when — The correction lands on the amount the reference actually shows.

Most common failure — Swinging past the target. Straight from the log: darks that were too hot one week were corrected into dead grey the next. This is the meta-check — it governs every other one on this page.

The standing habit — named, not solved.

7

Saturation

R7

Have the deepest darks been cooled and desaturated?

Passes when — A final cooling and desaturating pass over the deepest shadows.

Most common failure — Raw, saturated darks in the nasolabial folds and under the nose — they age and harden a face. Skipped because it is a refinement step, not a build step.

Recurring ×2.

8

Texture

R6 · R8

Does the hair have a density gradient, and do the highlights read as light?

Passes when — Hair dense at the root core, thinning to sparse individual strands with skin showing through; ‘black’ built from dark brown, navy and deep violet with a sheen. Highlights applied as broad, smooth passes.

Most common failure — A flat, filled black mass. And highlights drawn as directional lines — they read as scratches on the forehead, not as light.

Validated ×1 each.

How to use it

1 · Finish firstJudge a finished piece, not a work in progress. Half a drawing fails most of these by definition.
2 · Photograph it next to the referenceMost failures above are invisible on the paper in front of you and obvious in a photo — especially value and temperature.
3 · Take ONE into the next drawingNot all eight. One target, named before you start, judged pass or fail when you finish. That single constraint is the whole method — it is what makes a verdict mean anything.

Why there is no score

A number averages your mistakes into a mood. Pass/fail on one named target does the opposite: it survives being wrong, it is arguable, and it carries forward. Every verdict on this site is binary for the same reason — and the misses stay published.

This list is not finished

Eight checks is where fifteen weeks of grading has landed, not a law. Rules get added when a new failure repeats, and marked closing when they stop appearing. If a check here does not match what you see on your own paper, trust your paper.
